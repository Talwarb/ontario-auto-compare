How a Canadian mortgage payment is calculated

A mortgage payment is made up of principal (the amount borrowed) and interest (the cost of borrowing). A longer amortization generally reduces each scheduled payment but increases total interest if the rate stays the same. Our fixed-rate estimate converts a nominal annual rate compounded semi-annually into a rate for the selected payment frequency. The result does not include property tax, home insurance, closing costs or mortgage default insurance.

Example: Enter a $500,000 mortgage, 4.5% interest and 25 years in the mortgage calculator to compare monthly and biweekly payment amounts. This is a mathematical illustration, not a lender quote.

What reduces take-home pay in Ontario?

Gross salary is your pay before deductions. Net or take-home pay is what remains after deductions. For many employees, these include federal income tax, Ontario income tax, Canada Pension Plan contributions and Employment Insurance premiums. Ontario also has a surtax and health premium. Tax credits and deductions depend on your circumstances.

Our 2026 Ontario calculator uses published brackets and annual contribution limits but remains an approximation. For an employer payroll calculation, compare results against the CRA Payroll Deductions Online Calculator. Rates and rules can change by tax year.

Why GST/HST differs across provinces

Some provinces use harmonized sales tax (HST), while others apply 5% federal GST plus a separate provincial sales tax. For example, Ontario generally has 13% HST and Alberta has 5% GST. British Columbia generally has 5% GST plus a separate 7% PST on many taxable purchases. Quebec generally applies GST and QST. Not every item is taxable, and place-of-supply rules matter.

Our GST/HST calculator displays only federal GST or combined HST. If you are in a province with separate PST, RST or QST, that additional tax is not included. Verify a specific purchase using the CRA's current sales-tax guidance.

How loan interest changes total cost

A loan with a lower monthly payment is not always cheaper overall. Extending the repayment term can reduce the monthly payment while increasing the total interest paid. Our loan calculator assumes a fixed annual nominal interest rate divided into monthly periods, equal monthly payments and no fees. Actual lenders may use different conventions, fees or variable rates.

Understanding savings growth projections

Compounding means growth can itself generate further growth. Regular contributions may help savings accumulate, but actual investment returns can rise or fall. Our savings calculator uses an assumed constant annual return, monthly compounding and contributions at the end of each month. It excludes taxes, fees and inflation. The number shown is a scenario, not a forecast or guaranteed result.
